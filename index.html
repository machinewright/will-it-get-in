Buying a sofa, a wardrobe or a mattress? Measure it, and the tight spot on the way in: a hallway corner or a door. You'll see whether it gets through, exactly how to carry it (flat, tilted or on its end), and the longest one that would.

    How it works

    It treats the furniture as a box with the sizes you give, and your hallway and door as walls, a door frame and a ceiling. Then it searches for a way to move the box through: sliding it, turning it, lifting one end and tilting it, standing it on end, laying it on its back, in steps of 2.5 cm and 2.5°. Every move between steps is checked at points close enough together that the box keeps at least 1 cm from every wall, the door frame and the ceiling the whole way. So when it shows you a way, that way works for the box, with a centimetre to spare all round.

    When it finds no way, that is not quite a proof: a way could squeeze between its steps. For the case where it can be checked exactly (carrying a box flat round a corner, which has a known answer you can work out by hand) it agrees to within a centimetre, and it never claims a way where there isn't one. For a door it also uses a rule that is exact: at the moment the box's middle passes through the doorway, the line through its middle across the doorway is inside the opening, and every such line through a box is at least as long as its shortest side. So if the shortest side is wider than the doorway (less a centimetre each side), nothing will get it through.

    It assumes the box arrives at the tight spot from somewhere with room to turn it (outside, or a room), and that you can tip it there if the ceiling allows. It doesn't know about radiators, light fittings, banisters or a door that doesn't open fully: measure the narrowest point, with the door open. Stairs aren't covered yet.

    A real sofa isn't a box. If the box gets through, the sofa does too, since it fits inside the box. If the box doesn't, the sofa still might: arms and backs leave gaps that movers use (tilting the back through a door first, hooking an arm round the frame), and this tool can't see those. If the legs come off, enter the height without them.

    The code is on GitHub, with the tests: the search against the exact rule for flat carrying, and every way it finds checked again by separately written geometry.
